Intro

Since a project in high-school to make a Stewart Platform, I've been hooked on
the idea of building robots in which multiple stages have to work in unison to
successfully complete a task. Plenty of series systems exist in my life from my
3D printer to the various gantries of the electronics manufacturing line at my
workplace, but besides the common trivial case of synchronized stages there's
no real parallelism and I want to change that.

The goals of this project are going to be always in flux, whether it's because
of scope creep or that I lose interest in some subtask to complete the final
system. Regardless, I ultimately want to build a high-performance parallel-
cable robot, and learn a lot along the way. I also want to work myself to the
full extent of my knowledge, as well as pick up anything lost or bypassed over
the years. Many of my old CS skills were immensely useful to get me through
college, but I feel they rotted as I went to explore mechanical and electrical
engineering. The classes of my engineering degrees scraped the surface of
numerous deep subjects that I would somewhat explore in my own time, but never
was able to get deep into before the next assignments took away my time. Most
importantly though is the staggering amount of topics that I feel that are
important for me as an engineer to natively understand, intuit, and be capable
of applying, but were skipped over entirely. This continues to be true at work
where my chance to learn is largely limited by the design/debugging problems I
have to solve, and I want to break that cycle.

As I come up with new cohesive topics to discuss, I'll throw them into a page
and tack it on top of the list below. For the sake of posterity once I make a
post in this series, I won't change the webpage--use this to see how my ability
to make a webpage evolves from a spartan Berkshire-Hathaway-esque .html
document to something a bit easier on the eyes. This series starts with a post
on the Stewart Platform that started it all followed by a post that aggregates
all of my various musings about a parallel-cable robot over the years before I
really became serious about the execution. Posts thereafter will be tidbits
that I find interesting enough to talk about.
