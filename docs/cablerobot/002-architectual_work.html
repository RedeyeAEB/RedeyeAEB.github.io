(002) The Architecture of a Cable Robot

In my previous writeup, I talked about some of the earliest thoughts I had
towards making a parallel cable robot. Long story short, I wanted to push the
limits of what's possible with such a system, thought a lot about the cable
dynamics, and then got my hands on a massive capacitor bank before graduating
that fixes a lot of my power availability issues.

After graduating and finding myself with more time, I first went through my old
notes and compiled where my design was at, even if patchwork, and then reasses
how realisitic it was or how I could do better. Roughly speaking, I had been my
system would have a motion range of 1m, have a total inertial mass of 5kg, and
would be capable of a max speed of 100m/s. I opted to increase the size of the
gantry from a 1m cube to a 2m cube, yielding a significantly improved zone of
workability. After all, the angles involved as the payload approaches the walls
inherently means less accelerating force can be applied. It also means that the
mass has a longer distance to accelerate and decelerate, allowing for more
impressive velocity numbers. That being said, while I do want to play a bit of
specifications game, getting to that velocity number really means having an
impressive acceleration number, which is where the actual engineering challenge
applies.

After my gantry size was determined, I wanted to start to bound the problem
a bit more. To begin, I assumed that the power requirements of such a system
would be the major limitation. I also considered that such a gantry system is
inherently largely conservative--certainly it takes energy to accelerate a mass
to a considerable velocity, but that energy is returned to storage when the
motors regenerate the kinetic energy. Obviously, there will be losses--aero,
magnetic, conduction, switching, leakage to name a few--and these will have to
compensated for by pulling power from the grid. Thus, the useful movements of a
high-speed cable robot are limited by the losses overly discharging the
ultracapacitor bank faster than the grid converter can keep it topped up.
